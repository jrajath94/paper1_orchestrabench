## The Problem

Artificial intelligence (AI) -- the field of computer science focused on building systems that can perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence -- has advanced rapidly in recent years. One of the most significant developments has been the rise of large language models (LLMs), which are AI systems trained on vast amounts of text to understand and generate human language. These models power tools like chatbots, writing assistants, translation services, and code generation systems that millions of people use daily.

However, as AI systems are deployed to solve increasingly complex real-world problems, a single model working alone is often insufficient. This has led to the emergence of "agent orchestration" -- the challenge of coordinating multiple AI agents to work together effectively, much like a project manager coordinating a team of specialists. Imagine a hospital where a radiologist reads the scan, a surgeon plans the procedure, and an anesthesiologist manages pain control -- no single doctor does everything, and the quality of patient outcomes depends on how well these specialists communicate and coordinate.
